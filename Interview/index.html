The 10-minute protocol
  1. 0–2 min: restate, ask about edge cases, name the pattern.
  2. 2–4 min: state the approach and complexity aloud.
  3. 4–10 min: code cleanly, dry-run one example.

Targets: Easy ≤ 5 min · Medium ≤ 10 · Hard ≤ 20. No approach after 5 min? Read the hint, then redo cold after 3, 7 and 21 days (the R3/R7/R21 boxes). A problem counts as mastered only when a timed redo lands within target. Codeforces problems are rated by feel (E ≈ 800–1200, M ≈ 1300–1800), so treat their targets as guides.

What separates the best
  1. Pattern notebook. After every problem, write one line: the pattern, the trigger that revealed it, the complexity, and the trap you hit.
  2. Brute force first, always. State it, give its complexity, then optimize. Interviewers score the reasoning, not just the code.
  3. Three edge cases before coding: empty input, a single element, duplicates or maximum size.
  4. Blank-file recall each week: BFS, DFS, Dijkstra, union-find, topological sort, quickselect, heap operations, KMP.
  5. Upsolve contests. Solve the problem you missed before you sleep, then log why you missed it.
  6. Know one language cold. Heaps, deques, sorted containers and binary search from the standard library, with no lookups.
  7. Think aloud. Record yourself solving a problem and listen back. Silent speed doesn’t transfer to an interview.
  8. Weekly rhythm: 5 new problems under the clock, 5 redos, 1 contest, 1 mock, 1 notebook review.
  9. Shuffle topics in checkpoints. Recognizing the pattern without a label is the real skill.

Codeforces reality check

Phases 1–8 can take you to roughly Specialist or Expert (1400–1900) with disciplined timed reps. Candidate Master and above needs the Codeforces track below: 300+ rated problems, 2–3 live contests a week, upsolving every miss, number theory, advanced data structures and fluent C++. For hiring, though, contest rating matters less than the interview itself. A 1900 with strong system design, projects and clear communication usually beats a 2400 without them, and Fortune 500 loops reward explaining trade-offs, which contests never test.
