AI/ML in Medicine: undergraduate to PhD

A five-year plan for a computer science student aiming at a funded PhD in machine learning for healthcare, in the UK with NHS-linked groups or anywhere in the world. It trades certificate collecting for what admissions panels actually weigh: grades, a supervisor who vouches for you, validated research, and work other people can build on.

Ground rules

Your degree comes first: grades gate offers and funding. Six to eight hours a week in Years 1 and 2, ten to twelve in Years 3 and 4 (research placements excluded). Consistency over five years beats sprints.

Grades

A first or strong 2:1 (or a high GPA) keeps funding and most programmes open.

One academic who knows your work

Aim to have a strong referee by the summer of Year 3.

One validated preprint

Real external validation work, not another leaderboard score.

One reusable, citable tool

Documented, tested, licensed, with a DOI, so others can build on it.

Clinical grounding

Governance training, a clinician collaborator, and a project tested on a second dataset.

Skip these

SAS, Cloudera, Kafka and multi-cloud architect certificates, AGI years, "one paper a day", "50 projects". Clinical data lives in secure environments, so cloud certificates barely transfer.

Choose your track by the end of Year 2

With a software or testing mindset, EHR/clinical ML or trustworthy medical AI gives the best opportunity-to-competition ratio. Rigorous evaluation is scarce in this field and gets noticed.

Reporting standards to follow

TRIPOD+AI for prediction models, PROBAST+AI for appraising bias risk, CLAIM for imaging, CONSORT-AI / SPIRIT-AI for trials, DECIDE-AI for early clinical evaluation. Always split by patient, never by image or encounter.

PhD application timeline

In the UK the NHS partners with universities rather than awarding PhDs. Target university groups with NHS-embedded supervisors and funded programmes (UKRI AI CDTs, NIHR and Wellcome-linked routes, university studentships). Elsewhere, look at funded US departments and European or Canadian programmes. Dates shift every cycle, so verify before you plan around them.

When (Year 4)Do
Summer beforeShortlist 8 to 12 supervisors. Read their last three years of papers. Pair every ML supervisor with a clinical co-supervisor in your plan.
August to OctoberSend five-sentence emails: who you are, one specific paper of theirs, one project of yours with a link, a concrete idea, and a question about openings or funding.
October to DecemberSubmit CDT and studentship applications. US deadlines usually fall in December. Have three referees briefed and a 2 to 4 page proposal anchored in a problem a clinician told you matters.
January to MarchInterviews. Be ready to defend your validation choices and explain a failure.
February to AprilFunding decisions. If none land, take the Year 5 research-assistant route and reapply from a stronger position.

GitHub: one repo per project

A single giant journey repo is hard to cite and hard to build on. Use a profile README that links to pinned project repos, plus one learning-log repo for notes and exercises.

# ml-health-<project>/
README.md          # question, data, method, results, limitations
MODEL_CARD.md      # intended use and what it must NOT be used for
DATASHEET.md       # provenance, splits, how to request access
CITATION.cff
LICENSE
pyproject.toml     # or environment.yml
Dockerfile
Makefile           # make data / train / eval / report
configs/           # every experiment reproducible from YAML
src/<pkg>/         # data, features, models, evaluation, fairness
tests/             # includes: fails if any patient is in two splits
notebooks/         # exploration only
reports/           # TRIPOD+AI checklist, figures, preprint
docs/
Never commit patient data and never send credentialed data to external LLM APIs. Data use agreements such as MIMIC's forbid redistribution; share extraction code and access instructions instead.

Teaching syllabus

Blog first, a university talk second, video last. Cap teaching at roughly 10% of your weekly hours. Every post gets one diagram, one real example, and one failure story.
